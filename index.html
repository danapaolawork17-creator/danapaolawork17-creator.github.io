About

Engineering that leaves the screen

I'm a mechanical engineering student at Fresno State in the Finish in Five program, working toward my BS with an aerospace minor (Spring 2027) and my MS (Spring 2028). I started at West Hills Community College, where I earned my AA, before transferring to the Lyles College of Engineering.

My favorite work is where analysis meets real hardware: sizing a TEC to keep a laser from overheating, taking a part from a 3D print to a machined piece, or watching loads data come in from the control room during a flight. Right now I'm running stress analysis on a modified X-59 engine mount that I'll help test in the Flight Loads Lab.

NASA Armstrong, 2026

Laser thermal and vibration design

The laser in a quantum sensor prototype overheats past 40 °C, so I designed a thermal stack to pull heat away: a TEC with 6x margin over the laser's 12 W heat load, feeding a copper heat spreader and an aluminum optics plate.

I also designed the laser chassis in Creo, took it from 3D-printed fit checks to a machined part, and selected the vibration isolators so the sensor could handle flight-like vibration.

ME 154, Fresno State, Spring 2026

Double reduction spur gear speed reducer

With a team of five, I designed a two-stage gearbox for a commercial tractor that takes 6 hp at 2000 rpm down to 180 rpm, an 11.1:1 reduction split into two 3.33:1 stages (30 and 100 tooth gears).

We sized the gears with AGMA bending and contact stress analysis and the shafts with DE-Goodman fatigue criteria in MATLAB, targeting a 1.5 safety factor, infinite shaft life, and 5,000 hours on the gears and bearings. The design uses AISI 1045 steel gears and shafts, needle roller bearings, and a 6061-T6 aluminum housing. We modeled the full assembly in SolidWorks and checked it with FEA in both SolidWorks and Autodesk Inventor, then compared the results against our hand calculations.

  • SolidWorks
  • Inventor FEA
  • MATLAB
  • AGMA
  • Shaft fatigue
